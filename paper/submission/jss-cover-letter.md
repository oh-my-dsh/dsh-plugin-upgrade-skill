Dear Editors of the Journal of Systems and Software,

We are pleased to submit our manuscript, "Evaluating Agent Skills for Version-Specific Plugin Migration: A Retrospective Study," for consideration as a research paper in the Journal of Systems and Software.

Coding agents are increasingly equipped with packaged maintenance knowledge ("agent skills"), and such packages are usually judged by an aggregate benchmark score. Our manuscript asks whether a higher score shows that migration advice actually satisfies the contract of the target version. We study a shipped plugin-upgrade skill for a plugin-based agent framework through an archive of 64 answers to 16 static migration tasks and all 328 underlying criterion decisions.

The manuscript makes three contributions relevant to software maintenance and evolution:

1. An auditable within-configuration comparison that links every answer to its version-pinned task, original criterion judgments, and resource records.
2. A two-level account of grading: complete contract-stratified accounting of all decisions, a bounded review that locates judgments missing an operational requirement, and a blinded re-grading by judges from two other model families that measures how much the result depends on the judge.
3. Executable predicate and timer checks that separate a missed boundary defect from an alternative repair excluded by a narrower rubric, together with review questions that maintainers can apply to migration advice.

The study is reported as a retrospective case study, and its limitations (one framework, static diagnosis, no independent human annotation or control arm) are stated explicitly, together with planned follow-up studies. All tasks, agent outputs, grading records, review materials, and analysis scripts are openly available at https://github.com/oh-my-dsh/dsh-plugin-upgrade-skill, which we hope supports the journal's open-science practices.

A preprint of this manuscript is available on arXiv (arXiv:2609.30120). The manuscript has not been published elsewhere and is not under consideration by any other journal. All authors have approved the manuscript and agree with its submission to the Journal of Systems and Software. The authors declare no competing interests.
