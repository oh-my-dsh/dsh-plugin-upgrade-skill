Dear Editors of the Journal of Systems and Software,

Please consider “Evaluating Agent Skills for Version-Specific Plugin Migration: A Retrospective Study” as a research manuscript on AI-assisted software maintenance.

The paper examines a version-pinned plugin-upgrade skill through a focal archive of 64 answers on 16 tasks. It links complete accounting of 328 criterion judgments to a separate bounded output review. The study documents how higher recorded reward can coexist with a missed containment defect, and distinguishes that grading error from a lifecycle repair that functions under explicit host teardown but does not satisfy the narrower rubric. Extracted predicate tests and a controlled timer reconstruction support these distinctions. API, boundary, and liveness cases motivate concrete review questions for maintenance advice, without claiming that those checks have been evaluated as an intervention.

The contribution is an auditable software-maintenance case study connecting task contracts, answers, scoring decisions, and sensitivity analyses. Two cross-family LLM judges independently re-grade all 64 reports under the original rubric; both preserve a positive descriptive gain while changing its magnitude. Historical model comparisons are retained as appendix context. Original judgments remain intact, AI-assisted review and author-reported human follow-up are distinguished, and the manuscript explicitly limits claims about operational success and generalization.

We believe the study is relevant to readers evaluating AI assistance for software evolution and maintenance.
